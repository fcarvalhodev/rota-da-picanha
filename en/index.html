Picanha Route

Why didn't picanha get cheap?

In 2022, Lula promised “picanha and a cold beer”. Picanha is Brazil's favorite barbecue cut. Many people say it is still expensive. Here is the explanation, in simple words.

📊 See the summary in icons🎮 Play: The Truth Butcher

The short answer

The price of picanha depends mainly on how many cattle there are to sell and on how much the world pays for Brazilian beef. Public policies can influence costs, supply and the exchange rate, but they do not set the price on their own.

The 4 reasons

🐄

1. There are fewer cattle

2024🐄🐄🐄🐄🐄🐄many cows went to slaughter
2026🐄🐄🐄🐄🐄🐄fewer cattle left

In 2023 and 2024, when cattle were cheap, many ranchers sold their cows. The cow is the calf's mother. With fewer mothers, fewer calves were born. Today there are fewer cattle to sell, and what is scarce gets more expensive.

This back-and-forth is called the cattle cycle and it takes several years. No government can make a cow have a calf faster.

🚢

2. A lot of beef goes abroad

🥩🥩🥩🥩🥩🥩🥩🥩🥩🥩

Out of every 10 pieces of beef, 7 stay in Brazil and 3 go abroad. Half of what leaves goes to China.

The more beef goes away, the less is left at the butcher's here.

💵

3. Abroad they pay in dollars

🇧🇷Sell herepaid in reais
or
🌎Sell abroadpaid in dollars

The meatpacker can sell the beef here or to another country. If they pay more abroad, it will only sell here for the same price. That is why beef here follows the price abroad.

🌾

4. Raising cattle got more expensive

  • 🌽feed and corn
  • ⛽fuel and freight
  • ⚡energy
  • ☀️drought in the pastures

All of this went up. When it costs more to raise cattle, beef reaches the butcher's more expensive.

And the government?

✅ The government can

  • Remove taxes on beef. Complementary Law 214/2025 sets a zero IBS/CBS rate for meats in the basic food basket, under the conditions and transition of the tax reform; that does not mean all taxes are gone today.
  • Remove the tax on imported beef.

❌ The government cannot

  • Make more cattle be born from one year to the next.
  • Set the price China pays.
  • Make it rain on the pastures.

Is it true?

Not shown

“Picanha is expensive because of Lula.” There is no basis for attributing the price solely to the president. Cattle supply, exports, the exchange rate, demand, costs and public policies can all play a part.

Half true

“Lula promised picanha and did not deliver.” The promise was made. The data shown do not allow concluding how the historical purchasing power of the minimum wage has changed.

Depends on the measure

“Under Bolsonaro beef was cheap.” Picanha rose 38% in the IPCA between 2020 and 2022. A percentage increase does not, on its own, determine price level or purchasing power.

Half true

“Exporting beef leaves Brazilians without beef.” Less beef is left here, but 7 out of every 10 pieces still stay in Brazil.

In short

  • Picanha is expensive because there are fewer cattle, a lot of beef goes abroad and raising cattle got more expensive.
  • This does not depend only on the president.
  • Under Lula it rose less than under Bolsonaro, but it has still not got cheap.

Who sets the price of picanha

Beef is a commodity: the meatpacker can sell here or abroad. That makes the price here move together with the price abroad.

  1. The world pays in dollars. China, the US and others buy Brazilian beef at the international price.
  2. The dollar converts. With a strong dollar, each exported kilo brings the meatpacker more reais.
  3. The meatpacker compares. It only sells here if the price here gets close to what it would receive by exporting.
  4. Less is left for the domestic market. In the first half of 2025, 28.7% of the beef produced was exported, according to the reference cited.
  5. Cattle get more expensive. The arroba (15 kg of cattle) hit a nominal record of R$367.30 in April 2026, and the price reaches the butcher's.

What these numbers say

Family farming is 76.8% of rural properties, but 23% of the value of everything produced. It dominates cassava, milk and fruits such as strawberries and grapes, and is a minority in beans and cattle.

The MST (Landless Workers' Movement) harvested about 16,000 tonnes of organic rice in the 2022/23 season. That is little compared with the country's rice, but it is the largest organic volume in Latin America, according to Rio Grande do Sul's rice institute (Irga).

Verdict

The price of picanha depends more on cattle, the dollar and China than on who is in the presidential palace.

  • By the IPCA, picanha rose 38% under Bolsonaro and 7.3% under Lula through September 2026. By adjusted wholesale prices, it rose 25.7% since 2023. Both are true.
  • The promise was made. The minimum wage calculation presented here does not prove equivalent purchasing power to 2023.
  • Beef is a commodity: almost 30% goes abroad and the price here follows the price there. Policies can influence incentives and costs; the cattle cycle also depends on private decisions and natural conditions.
  • Family farming is essential for cassava, milk and fruit, but the 70% claim needs to define quantity, value, calories and destination of production to be verifiable.
